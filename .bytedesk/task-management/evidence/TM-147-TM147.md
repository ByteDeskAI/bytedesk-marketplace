## What actually happened during the merge

Both predicted failure modes occurred, which is why the sequence is not optional.

- **`eff264fa` conflicted modify/delete.** A live agent appended to the log between the clean
  checkout in step 2 and the merge in step 3 — the same race this change exists to end, occurring
  during the change that ends it. Resolved in favour of the deletion (the intent), file kept on disk.
- **`fd2b831f` was removed from disk by the merge**, exactly as the evidence said it would be, and
  was restored from the copy taken in step 1.

Copies were taken to two locations rather than one before anything was touched.

## The residual, restated because it applies here

A live agent's tmux `pipe-pane` holds an open descriptor. After restoration its future writes go to
the **unlinked inode**, not the restored file, until its pane is next re-piped. Both restored files
are therefore snapshots up to the merge.

`fd2b831f` is this integrator session's own pane and was live throughout, so its log is truncated at
this point by construction. That is the honest limit of the sequence, and one truncated tail is a
smaller cost than a merge that silently drops code — which is what a tracked `pane.log` had already
caused once.

## Why this was held for the operator rather than merged with the rest

It deletes files another session committed and changes ignore rules repository-wide. The argument
for doing it strengthened during the session — the silent bad merge is a direct consequence — and
that is precisely when it stops being the integrator's call, because the integrator is the party it
benefits.
