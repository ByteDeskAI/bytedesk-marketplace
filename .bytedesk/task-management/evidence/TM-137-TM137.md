# TM-137 — v2 drafted by enumeration, awaiting countersignature

Committed at `4d5adc2`. **Draft. The producer still emits `schemaVersion: 1`.**

## The decision, and the option that was rejected

The operator ruled: **enumerate the roles in v2, do not reverse §9.5.**

The rejected alternative was `roleName`-driven rendering, which the task itself suggested as
preferable. It reverses a clause the gateway countersigned in `PRESENCE-HEADER-ADDENDUM.md` §9.5 —
*"`roleName` is display metadata. Never group on it, never label 'Team lead' from it."*

`roleName` is producer-supplied free text carrying whatever the agent library says. It was signed
**specifically** as a field a consumer must not derive labels from, and that is what lets the
producer put an unrecognised role there without a consumer acting on it. Reversing it would make
every future library role a silent wire change with no version to negotiate — the property the
freeze exists to prevent.

Enumeration keeps adding a role a version change. That is the cost, and it is the intended one:
**a closed vocabulary is what makes a rendering rule checkable.**

## What v2 opens

| field | v1 | v2 |
|---|---|---|
| `repoRole` | `lead` \| `reviewer` \| `member` | adds `designer`, `image-gen` |
| `runRole` | seven tokens | adds `image-gen` |
| `schemaVersion` | `1` | `1` or `2` |

"Team lead" is still rendered from `repoRole === "lead"` and nothing else — opening the set adds
siblings to that rule rather than weakening it. §9.5 stands unchanged in v2.

## The validator does not reimplement the contract

`validate_presence_v2.py` imports the **frozen** v1 validator, overrides exactly the two
vocabularies, and delegates every remaining rule to the frozen code.

A forked copy could drift on the six-tuple, the counters, the bounds or the pairing and nobody would
notice for months. Here drift is impossible by construction, and the difference between v1 and v2
acceptance is provably the two sets plus the version number.
